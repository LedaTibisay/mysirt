Background

MySirt is the result of long-term engineering work focused on transforming structured domain definitions into executable operational systems. The Execution Engine evolved through continuous engineering, real-world deployments, and progressive architectural refinement rather than short-term experimentation.

Origins

The modeling language originated in the 1990s and has evolved across multiple technological generations while preserving its core architectural principles.

The initial architectural concepts were developed within a collaborative engineering context centered on formal system modeling and execution logic. The foundational idea was simple but demanding: system behavior should emerge from structured definitions rather than from manually implemented features.

Technologies changed multiple times over the following decades, but the fundamental principles remained constant. MySirt does not originate from recent AI or low-code trends; it reflects more than three decades of continuous architectural evolution.

Progressive Formalization

Over time, the Execution Engine evolved through incremental architectural refinement:

  • Formalization of the domain-specific language
  • Implementation of structural regeneration mechanisms
  • Integration of rule execution within the generative process
  • Stabilization of a layered execution architecture

Each phase strengthened internal coherence and reduced dependency on ad-hoc implementation patterns.

The architecture did not remain static. When real projects revealed recurring patterns, those patterns were generalized and new abstractions were integrated into the DSL. The DSL and the Model Execution Engine evolved together while maintaining coherence with the original architectural principles.

Real-World Validation

The engine has been validated through systems actually used in production across multiple domains, including administrative systems, operational management platforms, and structured workflow applications.

These real-world deployments directly informed architectural evolution, reinforcing determinism, regeneration stability, and model-driven execution consistency. Some of these examples are documented in the Operational Evidence section of the site.

Engineering Principles

  • Model-first system definition
  • Deterministic interpretation over manual feature coding
  • Structural coherence through regeneration
  • Explicit formalization of rules and responsibilities
  • Separation between specification and execution layers

Continuity

MySirt is not the result of a short-term research project. It represents more than three decades of continuous architectural evolution. Each operational project contributed to refining both the DSL and the Model Execution Engine, while the fundamental principles remained unchanged throughout its evolution.
